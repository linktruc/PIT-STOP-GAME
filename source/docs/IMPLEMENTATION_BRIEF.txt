2. CORE F1 PIT STOP ROLES AND WORK DEPENDENCIES
================================================================================
CORE PLAYABLE SLOTS — EXACTLY 15 (either HUMAN or BOT)
Wheel corners: FL = front left, FR = front right, RL = rear left, RR = rear right.
- FL_GUNNER / FR_GUNNER / RL_GUNNER / RR_GUNNER: ONE person per corner does two separate sequential tasks, GUN_LOOSEN and GUN_TIGHTEN, using a wheel gun on the single central wheel nut.
- FL_WHEEL_OFF / FR_WHEEL_OFF / RL_WHEEL_OFF / RR_WHEEL_OFF: remove the old wheel when it is safe and the nut is loosened.
- FL_WHEEL_ON / FR_WHEEL_ON / RL_WHEEL_ON / RR_WHEEL_ON: mount the replacement wheel once the old wheel is removed.
- FRONT_JACK: lift the front at the start; lower the front after both FL/FR are secure.
- REAR_JACK: lift the rear at the start; lower the rear after both RL/RR are secure.
- RELEASE_CONTROLLER: verify completion via the graph and activate green light/release the car as the final core action.
Total = 4 + 4 + 4 + 2 + 1 = 15. Exactly one HUMAN per assigned role; other core slots become bots. NEVER ask a human to cover two roles just because the room has fewer members. A gunner/jack still performs its TWO steps within that ONE assigned role.

BOT-ONLY AMBIENT CREW (NOT PART OF SCORING DAG)
- Two stabilizers: stabilize the chassis, animate contextually as the car is lifted/lowered, step aside on release.
- Two front-wing inspection/adjustment crew: ambient check in a standard four-wheel-change round; only actually adjust wing in a future separate scenario, not this default race.
- Two to three safety/spares crew: stand by, observe, communicate, move tools. No pretend fires, mechanical failures or made-up mandatory confirmations. These background characters can keep moving without adding delays or blocking the release.
- Therefore the visible pit crew is 21–22 characters. 15 core operator characters exist at all times, controlled by humans or bots according to assignment, PLUS ambient bot crew. Actors should be tagged HUMAN / BOT where useful; the positions and animations should not disappear when a human slot is absent.

FIXED DAG AND PARALLELISM
- GO unlocks FRONT_LIFT and REAR_LIFT. At least one is a human action.
- Front corners FL and FR require FRONT_LIFT; rear corners RL and RR require REAR_LIFT. Front and rear jacking happen concurrently when both are human; if one jack is a bot, see Section 5's EXACT SYNC JACK RULE.
- Within EACH corner independently: GUN_LOOSEN -> WHEEL_OFF -> WHEEL_ON -> GUN_TIGHTEN.
- Do NOT add a global stage barrier requiring every wheel's loosen task to finish before any wheel can start wheel-off. Each corner proceeds when its OWN prerequisites clear; all four corners can overlap.
- FRONT_LOWER can start only when the gun has tightened BOTH front wheels; REAR_LOWER only after BOTH rear wheels are tightened.
- GREEN_RELEASE can start only after BOTH lowering tasks are done, all 4 wheel nuts secured, and the graph indicates release safe. Release controller (human or bot) completes the final action and fixes Pit Stop Time.
- The global task graph is the same for a team of 4 or 15; only human/bot ownership changes.
- Display only meaningful DAG nodes and dependencies. Ambient stabilizers, optional wing checks and spare/safety crew animate in 3D but are not extra required graph steps.
- All server states: LOCKED, AVAILABLE, ACTIVE/AWAITING_ACTION, DONE. Each step stores actor role, human/bot owner, enable timestamp, completion timestamp; visual animations faithfully reflect server transitions.

5. ADAPTIVE BOT TIMING WITH PARALLEL NO-BOT-BOTTLENECK RULE
================================================================================
This is a GAMEPLAY EQUALIZATION MECHANIC, not a literal description of all real F1 team behavior. The correctness of this mechanic is a top priority. Code it with deterministic transactional server events and thorough tests, not with frontend-only timers.

TIME SAMPLES
- Reaction time of one HUMAN core ACTION = timestamp of successful server-authorized action click minus its server-recorded AVAILABLE timestamp (do not include time while dependencies were blocked). Store nonnegative bounded/sanity-checked values in milliseconds. For one player with two actions (gunner/jack), record each click separately as a new sample.
- `current_human_mean_ms = sum(valid_human_reaction_ms_so_far) / number_of_completed_human_actions_so_far`. Average is over ACTION samples, not over number of players. Never read samples from the future, use fixed assumptions, or use the opponent team's data. It updates after EVERY new human completed action.
- Bot-only ambient crew does not affect samples or scoring time.

SPECIAL FIRST-JACK SYNCHRONIZATION — OVERRIDES NORMAL BOT DELAY
- EVERY round must allocate at least ONE jack slot to a human.
- At GO, both front/rear LIFT nodes are logically unlocked. If one jack is HUMAN and the other jack is BOT, the bot lift enters `WAIT_FOR_HUMAN_JACK_SYNC` (do not apply any default bot duration, and do not let it finish before the human).
- The moment the human jack validly performs its LIFT, record that real reaction (for example 400ms), and in the SAME atomic server state transition mark BOTH the human lift and bot lift DONE at the SAME timestamp. Trigger synchronized visual completion/lift animations. Both front and rear wheel branches become available together; there is no extra 400ms for the bot after the human completes.
- If BOTH jacks are human, both lift tasks are independently eligible from GO; whichever clicks first creates the first real sample. Neither is artificially forced to wait for the other; each axle unlocks when its own jack completes.
- Do not apply this first-jack exception to the later FRONT_LOWER/REAR_LOWER stage automatically; they may be concurrently grouped and use the parallel bot rules below if relevant.

NORMAL BOT DEADLINE
- When a core bot task becomes AVAILABLE after at least one human sample, store `eligible_at` and a snapshot of `current_human_mean_ms` and schedule its baseline completion at `eligible_at + snapshot_mean_ms` (plus any explicitly modeled and uniformly applied gameplay handling, if needed). Snapshot of the baseline does not retroactively change when new human clicks are observed.
- A bot-only singleton/sequential stage follows that baseline deadline, respecting dependencies. If a stage has NO humans at all, bot tasks should also normally use their baseline delay. Do NOT make all bots instantaneous.

PARALLEL COHORTS AND EARLY COMPLETION CAP — **MUST IMPLEMENT**
- Define meaningful PARALLEL ACTION COHORTS, not arbitrary physical proximity or unrelated nodes:
  * `LIFT` contains FRONT_LIFT + REAR_LIFT, with the unique opening sync rule above.
  * `GUN_LOOSEN` contains four corners' loosen tasks.
  * `WHEEL_OFF` contains four corners' remove-old-wheel tasks.
  * `WHEEL_ON` contains four corners' fit-new-wheel tasks.
  * `GUN_TIGHTEN` contains four corners' tighten tasks.
  * `LOWER` contains FRONT_LOWER + REAR_LOWER.
  * `GREEN_RELEASE` is a SINGLETON and uses normal mean when bot controlled.
- These are *logical peer cohorts* for fairness, NOT global barriers. The wheel DAG still advances independently per corner whenever local prerequisites are satisfied. Cohorts can have different AVAILABLE times due to earlier per-corner dependencies.
- When a bot peer becomes AVAILABLE, keep its baseline mean deadline. A bot may complete at its own deadline earlier than the humans (that's fine).
- BUT if a cohort contains one or more REAL HUMAN tasks in this round and **ALL those human tasks in that cohort are DONE**, immediately fast-forward any remaining ELIGIBLE/AVAILABLE bot tasks of the same cohort to DONE in the very same atomic server transition, with completion timestamp no later than that last real human peer completion timestamp (or the bot's eligibility timestamp if it only just became available later). In other words, eligible bot peers must NEVER leave a parallel group waiting after the humans doing those parallel actions have finished.
- If the last human in a cohort finishes after a bot's baseline deadline, that bot can already be DONE; never delay it waiting for human peers.
- If human peers complete faster than a bot's baseline reference, the eligible bot completes immediately at the final human completion event — without waiting for its old baseline deadline. This is a deliberate exception to the V4 immutable-deadline rule.
- Only speed up nodes whose OWN prerequisites are already satisfied. Do not mark a future wheel-on DONE while that corner's wheel-off is not yet done. If a newly eligible bot node enters a cohort whose human peers are already all DONE, fast-forward that newly eligible bot as soon as it becomes eligible, in causal order. This resolves disparate unlock times without breaking the DAG. A chain of dependent BOT-only nodes may still require sequential mean delays for stages without human peers; do not fabricate an impossible completed wheel.
- If a cohort has ZERO human members, there is nobody to synchronize with. All bots use snapshot mean delays normally, preserving sequencing and causal correctness.
- In a mixed cohort, a bot must never be the *final outstanding ready/eligible peer action* after all humans in that same cohort are finished. Do not treat unrelated future DAG nodes as peers.
- The mechanic is enforced by the backend and applied to all subscribers (players + global spectator), including resuming missed events and concurrent requests. Use exact, auditable effective timestamps.
- Distinguish ACTION SERVER COMPLETION from visually asynchronous animation length. Human action can complete logically at the valid click, triggering a visual animation; do not add inconsistent long animation durations that make bot sync appear wrong. Client animation must align/recover from server state and never override logical completion.

ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLES — ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
EXAMPLE A — FIRST JACK:
- FRONT_JACK = HUMAN, REAR_JACK = BOT. GO at t=0. Human clicks LIFT at t=0.400s. Both LIFT nodes DONE at t=0.400s exactly. First mean sample = 400ms. The rear bot does NOT finish at t=0.800s.
EXAMPLE B — PARALLEL WHEEL-ON FAST-FORWARD:
- Reference mean is 400ms. Three human WHEEL_ON actions and one eligible bot WHEEL_ON are simultaneously unlocked. Human peers finish after 150ms, 200ms and 250ms. Bot must finish no later than 250ms after common unlock, synchronized to the last human completion. It must NOT wait 400ms.
EXAMPLE C — BOT FINISHES BEFORE HUMANS:
- Reference mean 400ms. One human peer takes 800ms; bot peer can complete at t=400ms. Do not hold bot until 800ms.
EXAMPLE D — ALL-BOT COHORT:
- All four GUN_LOOSEN actions happen to be bots. They take their eligible-time baseline (current human mean), with ordinary causal unlocks. No magic instantaneous completion.
EXAMPLE E — NO EARLY CAUSAL VIOLATIONS:
- Human WHEEL_ON peer finishes before a bot WHEEL_ON at another corner becomes eligible (its old wheel has not yet been removed). The later bot cannot violate its WHEEL_OFF predecessor. The instant it becomes eligible, if all human WHEEL_ON peers are already finished, it can complete right then, but not before.
EXAMPLE F — BOT RELEASE SINGLETON:
- RELEASE_CONTROLLER is a bot; when both jacks have lowered, it waits the current human mean and then signals green. No human peers exist in GREEN_RELEASE to force instant completion.
EXAMPLE G — MIXED LOWER:
- FRONT_LOWER human, REAR_LOWER bot, both eligible at the same instant. Human finishes in 220ms, mean baseline for bot was 450ms. Rear bot lower also finishes by 220ms. This is distinct from first-jack sync but has the same outcome in this example.

IMPORTANT FAIRNESS NOTE
This rule prevents bots from becoming the final bottleneck *within a directly comparable parallel action group*, while still assigning realistic adaptive delays to bot-only and sequential work. It does NOT mathematically guarantee identical outcomes for 4-person and 15-person teams because their skill variability and cohort composition differ. Do not secretly normalize or tamper with displayed Pit Stop Time to compensate.
